Progress in women's health research has long been limited by a shortage of models that reflect human biology. Animal models such as mouse, rat, and rabbit differ substantially from human anatomy and follow a shorter, simpler hormonal cycle, which weakens their relevance to conditions like menopause, hormone therapy, drug side effects, and cancer. Growing human cells in the laboratory offers an alternative, but flat two-dimensional cultures cannot capture the layered, structured nature of real tissue.

Our lab builds three-dimensional tissue constructs from human cells housed in microfluidic devices, an approach WFIRM has applied across many organ systems, including lung, heart, liver, and brain. We are now extending this work to create a vagina-on-a-chip: a layered human vaginal tissue model within a device that provides dynamic control over fluid flow, hormone levels, and environmental exposures.

The model draws on our earlier lung-on-a-chip, in which the major cell types of the human lung were grown at an air-liquid interface to reproduce features such as mucus production and beating cilia. For the vaginal model, tightly connected, tissue-like cells are grown on a porous membrane that allows independent fluid access to each surface. Working with MatTek Corporation, we have integrated a commercially available vaginal tissue product into our custom device, and we are developing methods to incorporate patient-derived cells. Custom software and digital flow control let us raise and lower several hormones over time to emulate the natural 28-day cycle.

This platform offers a controllable window into human vaginal tissue function, supporting safer hormone and drug testing and more personalized care. Early applications include menopause, birth control interactions, hormone therapy, autoimmune conditions, and radiotherapy-induced tissue damage. Future versions aim to add patient-specific cells and connect with models of the ovary, cervix, and wider reproductive tract to study the female reproductive system as a whole.
